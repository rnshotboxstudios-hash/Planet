# Planet — original product scope

Build an educational autonomous workflow factory for ages 10+. A student gives a mission; a renameable Overseer plans the team, equips agents, supplies workstations, connects handoffs, and explains each step in clear language. Avoid making students discover hidden capability/equipment dependencies. The Overseer should diagnose failures, propose supported repairs, add agents/tools when needed, and preserve completed work during recovery. Room placement should eventually adapt and expand instead of trapping the student in layout errors.

Visuals: original anime collectible-style agents in Minecraft-inspired, smoothly bevelled octagonal warehouse rooms, thick readable borders, green/wood/stone materials, alien landscapes outside. StarNet-style and Sims-like ambient walking, glances, pauses, visits, and work gestures make the space feel alive. Ambient activity never counts as task success or earnings.

Growth: multiple rooms, floors, buildings and eventually a planet; player and agent progression to level 100. Year-four concepts illustrate twenty rooms across five two-floor buildings. The investor reel is exactly forty seconds: Year 1 original workshop, Year 4 master workshop, Year 4 district, Year 4 planet, ten seconds each. It is animated artwork, not live footage of an implemented twenty-room app.

Business goal: real useful deliverables and an eventually connected, age-appropriate online business system with guardian review and supported teen-account payouts. Payments and revenue must be verified with providers before claims of functionality. No guaranteed earnings or error-free execution claims.

Current next implementation priorities: durable mission planning and tool execution; mission-specific artifacts; automatic capability assignment and preflight validation; bounded recovery; multiple persistent rooms with clear navigation; guardian controls and real deployment integrations. Use a supported server model rather than inventing access to a named future model.
